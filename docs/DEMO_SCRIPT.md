# Demo and pitch

## The one-sentence pitch

**Pulse is a private AI coach for moments when you cannot look at a screen.**

## 90-second live demo

### 0:00–0:12 — problem

“When you are presenting, you need real-time feedback—but checking a screen destroys the presence you are trying to create. Pulse puts that feedback on your wrist instead.”

### 0:12–0:28 — prompt and plan

Show the prompt:

“Coach my 60-second pitch. Cue me at 30 seconds, if I look down for two seconds, and if I leave the presentation zone.”

Show the generated, validated rule cards. Emphasize: “The prompt configures a bounded coaching plan; every cue is visible and explainable.”

### 0:28–0:62 — magic loop

One presenter wears Pulse and begins a short pitch. A second person or the projector shows the camera overlay, timer, and event log.

Trigger the first reliable condition: at 30 seconds, the wristband gives a short vibration and the screen logs it. Then intentionally look down or step outside the marked zone long enough to trigger one more, clearly distinct cue. The presenter visibly corrects their behavior without looking at the screen.

### 0:62–0:78 — why it matters

“The feedback stays private. The camera is processed locally and not stored. Today it is Presenter Coach; the same prompt-to-plan loop can power focus, practice, and task-timing skills.”

### 0:78–0:90 — close

“Notifications demand your eyes. Pulse gives you your eyes back.”

## Blackjack joke

Use only after the core product is clear, and only if the tone of the room suits it:

“An 18-year-old in Miami immediately asked whether this could learn blackjack. It could make a great practice mode—but we built it for the moments where looking at a screen is the actual problem.”

Do not demonstrate casino use or portray Pulse as a real-time gambling aid.

## Demo reliability rules

- Start every demonstration with a motor test that the audience can see in the event log.
- Use a clearly taped presentation zone and a bright, front-facing camera position.
- Use one rehearsed face/zone condition; never wait for a subjective AI judgment.
- Project the dashboard because an audience cannot feel the wrist vibration.
- Keep a 60–90 second backup video ready, but do not lead with it.
- If camera tracking fails, demo the timer cue, then explain the local camera rule and show the recording. Do not fake an event.

## Questions to be ready for

**“Is this just a timer?”**  No. The timer is the reliable first rule. The product is an editable, camera-aware coaching plan with haptic output.

**“Can it do anything from a prompt?”**  Not arbitrarily. This prototype converts prompts into a small set of validated sensing and haptic rules. That is how we keep it fast, transparent, and dependable.

**“Why not a phone notification?”**  Looking at a phone during a pitch is the failure mode. The signal must be private and screenless.

**“Is it a safety or medical device?”**  No. Pulse is a coaching prototype and makes no safety, accessibility, or health claims.
